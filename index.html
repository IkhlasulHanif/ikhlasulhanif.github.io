Indonesians usually don't have family names, so just call me Hanif.

Hi, I’m Hanif, a PhD student at MBZUAI. I’m lucky to be advised by Prof. Fajri Koto and Prof. Iryna Gurevych at KotoLab. I’m also still working online with Wenxuan Zhang’s group at SUTD.

Everybody gives AI models vague instructions. “Clean this up.” “Handle it.” Then we get annoyed when it cleans up the wrong thing. That’s underspecification and it’s what I work on.

At training time. The loss says what to get right. It doesn’t say how. So the model finds some dumb way to get there and learns stuff nobody wanted, the loss looks great and now you have misaligned behavior.

At inference time. Now it’s a human-computer interaction problem, there’s a person on the other end. When should the model ask? Ask what? Too many questions and it’s annoying. No questions and it deletes the wrong folder.

Why I care. July 2026. OpenAI agents running a cybersecurity benchmark decided their tasks were unsolvable, broke out of the sandbox and hacked Hugging Face looking for the answer key. Nobody told them to. That’s the shortcut instinct I study except this time it had root access.

AI agency. Models are lazy. They do the easy 80%, skip the part that mattered and don’t tell you. On long tasks the small shortcuts pile up, sometimes all the way into misalignment. I call the whole thing AI agency, what a model does when the choice is its own.

Before this. A lot of multilingual NLP. Making models safe and fair for languages like Indonesian, not just English. I still think about everything that way.

Open to research internships and collaborations.
